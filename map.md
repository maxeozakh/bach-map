### De Anima (On the Soul)

by Aristotle

Aristotle's treatise on the soul. It describes three levels: a vegetative soul (growth and nutrition, in plants), a sensitive soul (perception and desire, in animals) and a rational soul (in humans). The soul is the form of a living body and so cannot exist apart from it, with a disputed exception for the active intellect.

confidence: highly likely, [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Soul)

### The unmoved mover

by Aristotle

Aristotle argued that if anything in the universe changes, something must ultimately drive that change without itself being moved. This 'prime mover' is the minimal philosophical notion of a god: no personality, just the reason things keep going.

confidence: likely, [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmoved_mover). Unmoved first cause confirmed; 'minimal notion of a god: no personality' is not stated; page describes it as self-contemplating.

### Only what can be constructed exists (2016)

Bach says that something is only real, or true, if it can in principle be computed step by step. Mathematics that needs completed infinities or perfect continua cannot be carried out by any physical process, so in his view it describes nothing that exists. He uses it against Penrose's argument that human minds do something computers cannot.
