## Hyatt Regency, San Francisco Airport | Burlingame, CA, USA

The Association for the Advancement of Artificial Intelligence is pleased to present the 2026 Spring Symposium Series to be held Tuesday, April 7 – Thursday, April 9, 2026 at Hyatt Regency, San Francisco Airport in Burlingame, California. The Spring Symposium Series is an annual set of meetings run in parallel at a common site. It is designed to bring colleagues together in an intimate forum while at the same time providing a significant gathering point for the AI community. The two-and-a-half-day format of the series allows participants to devote considerably more time to feedback and discussion than typical one-day workshops. It is an ideal venue for bringing together new communities in emerging fields.

Symposia generally range from 40–75 participants each. Participation was open to active participants as well as other interested individuals on a first-come, first-served basis. Each participant was expected to attend a single symposium. 

The program includes the following symposia: 

- Advances in AI-Enabled Tactical Autonomy: from Sensing to Execution

## Advances in AI-Enabled Tactical Autonomy: from Sensing to Execution

Uncrewed Aerial Systems (UAS) are increasingly central to modern defense, security, and disaster response operations. To perform effectively in contested, dynamic, or communication-denied environments, these systems must achieve true tactical autonomy, the ability to sense, reason, decide, and act robustly under uncertainty. While AI and autonomy research has produced powerful algorithms in simulation and laboratory settings, a persistent gap remains between theoretical advances and their translation to field-deployable systems. This symposium bridges that gap by convening researchers and practitioners from AI, autonomy, sensing, and systems engineering. The main objectives are to: (1) highlight theoretical advances in AI, perception, planning, and control applicable to tactical autonomy; (2) identify real-world constraints that limit the transition of AI algorithms to practice; (3) foster interdisciplinary collaboration between algorithm developers, system integrators, and end-users; and (4) define ethical, explainability, and assurance frameworks for trustworthy tactical autonomy.

### Topics

- AI Planning and Decision-Making Under Real-Time Constraints
- Learning and Adaptation Under Uncertainty
- Sensor Fusion and Perception in Degraded or Adversarial Environments
- Explainable and Verifiable AI for Autonomy
- Artificial Reasoning for Trustworthy Autonomy
- Human–Autonomy Teaming and Operator Oversight
- Multi-Agent Coordination and Swarm Intelligence
- Robustness, Safety, and Resilience in Tactical Operations

### Format of Symposium

The symposium will feature a mix of invited keynote presentations from leading researchers in AI and tactical autonomy, contributed paper presentations organized by thematic tracks (AI, sensing, reasoning, control, trust etc), interactive panel discussions on research bottlenecks and ethical considerations, and student-poster sessions for in-depth technical exchange. Special emphasis will be placed on interactive demonstrations of autonomous UAS capabilities and small-group discussions focused on defining community roadmaps for transitioning research to operational systems. The symposium is open to all interested participants.

### Submission Requirements

Full papers (6-8 pages) short/position/poster papers (2-4 pages), in AAAI format. Submissions should describe theoretical advances, system implementations, field test results, benchmark datasets, or position statements on research directions. Supplementary materials (videos, code, datasets) are encouraged.

Submissions will be accepted through EasyChair: https://easychair.org/conferences?conf=sss26

## AI in Business: Intelligent Transformation and Management

AI in Business aims to bring together academics and practitioners to examine how artificial intelligence is reshaping businesses. Since its inception, AI is now commonly found in a wide range of business domains, ranging from operations, customer engagement, and innovation. Generative AI opened the door and delivered real productivity gains, but the deeper transformation is happening in autonomous agents that execute complex, multi-step processes without constant human oversight. This AI symposium will provide a platform to explore both the strategic implications of AI as well as the technological implications of AI.

### Topics

We invite contributions that align with the symposium’s theme, including but not limited to: Strategic Planning; Sales/Demand Forecasting; Predictive and Prescriptive Analytics; Operations, Production, and Supply Chain Management; Automation and Robotics; Internet of Things (IoT) and AI Integration; Finance and Economics; Marketing; Human Resources and Behavioral Analytics; LLM Agents; Ethics and Governance; Healthcare; Other related topics in AI and business applications.

### Format of Symposium

The symposium will feature a diverse program that includes invited keynote speakers, contributed talks, tutorial/workshops, social activities, and panel discussion sessions.

### Attendance

Attendance is open to academics, practitioners, and policymakers working in the fields of AI, machine learning, and data science, as well as graduate students and researchers specializing in AI applications for business or related disciplines.

### Submission Requirements

Submissions are through the AAAI Official EasyChair platform (https://easychair.org/conferences?conf=sss26). All accepted papers will be included in the AAAI Spring 2026 proceedings.

## Machine Consciousness: Integrating Theory, Technology, and Philosophy

Recent AI advances have forced us to confront the possibility of consciousness, beyond intelligence, in machines. Asking “Can machines be conscious?” forces us to answer “How can we define consciousness in a formal way/what is the causal structure of consciousness?” (theory, formal frameworks), “How would consciousness be implemented?” (technical realization), “How can we determine that a given system is conscious?” (measurement/attribution), and “What status would consciousness lend to machines?” (ethics). These questions cannot be pursued in isolation. Theoretical advances have practical implications; implementation constraints inform which theories matter; ethical considerations shape research priorities; measurement challenges reveal gaps in theoretical understanding. Over 2.5 days, participants will work through theoretical, empirical, technical, and normative dimensions of machine consciousness, with each session building on previous ones. The format identifies concrete problems at the intersections where progress requires integration across disciplines.

### Topics

We welcome submissions on theoretical foundations (mathematical models, computational theories including IIT, Global Workspace, Attention Schema Theory, cognitive architectures); measurement and attribution problems (methods for detecting consciousness in artificial systems, limitations of behavioral testing, criteria for attribution); implementation challenges (engineering systems that satisfy theoretical criteria, role of embodiment and autonomy, architectural requirements); normative implications (ethical frameworks, moral status and rights, policy and governance); and integration across these domains (theory-measurement gaps, how implementation constraints inform theory, interdisciplinary frameworks).

### Format

The symposium uses progressive integration over 2.5 days. Each half-day focuses on one core topic with keynote presentations, selected talks, and structured integration discussions connecting to previous sessions. The final half-day is dedicated to collaborative working groups synthesizing insights across all domains.

### Attendance

Open to all interested participants. In line with AAAI policy, all presenters must attend in person.

### Submission Requirements

We invite full technical papers (6-8 pages excluding references), extended abstracts (2 pages including brief speaker biography), and position papers (4-6 pages excluding references). Submissions should follow AAAI formatting guidelines. Papers addressing cross-cutting themes or explicitly connecting multiple symposium topics are particularly encouraged. Submissions due January 9, 2026.

## Machine Learning and Knowledge Engineering for Knowledge-Grounded Semantic Agents (MAKE 2026)

MAKE 2026 aims to bring together a diverse group of researchers and practitioners to explore integrating machine learning and knowledge engineering in the development of knowledge-grounded semantic agents. The goal is to semantically advance agent architectures—including LLM, VLM, VLA, LxM, and multi-agent systems—to become knowledge-grounded, trustworthy, robust, interpretable, and capable of human-aligned reasoning and decision-making across diverse applications.

### Topics

- Machine Learning, Deep Learning, and Neural Networks
- Knowledge Engineering, Representation, and Reasoning
- Trustworthy, Commonsense, and Explainable AI

- Hybrid (Human–Artificial) Intelligence and Human-in-the-Loop AI

## Multi-Agent System Safety and Teamwork (MASST): Building Better AI

Multi-agent systems (MAS) will help unleash AI from the solitary confinement of standalone applications so they can act in the wider world, functioning safely and effectively within networks of people, “tools,” and other AIs. Rather than relying on a single vendor to supply AI capabilities, open standards undergirding the MAS approach are intended to foster a burgeoning marketplace of ideas and implementations.

The purpose of the symposium is to bring together the diverse, emerging community of researchers who have begun to discover scientific and engineering principles and technologies to enable safer and more effective collaboration within multi-agent systems of all sizes and forms. Our focus is not so much on better regulation and use of AI as it is on principles to enable the building of better AI— at both the component and system levels.

### Topic 

The topic of the workshop is Principles, Aspirations, and Examples for MAS Safety and Teamwork. Abstracts should not only briefly describe a particular approach or implementation but should also: 1. Summarize principles that undergird the approach and make it amenable to more general application; 2. Describe similarities and differences with at least one other approach or implementation, preferably originating from a separate research group.

### Submissions

Two-page abstracts including relevant publications and names and contact information for all authors should be submitted for review to Jeffrey M. Bradshaw (jbradshaw@ihmc.org). The subject line for the message should include the words “AAAI 2026 MASST Spring Symposium Submission.” Due date: February 27.

### Symposium Format

In the first part of the symposium, each session will consist of a group of presentations, followed by a general discussion period. The second part will consist of small group discussions on selected topics, followed by presentations and discussions by the groups of each topic.

### Attendance

Attendance is open to all, with priority given to those with submissions accepted for presentation.

### Publication

## Will AI Light Up Human Creativity or Replace It?: Toward Well-Being AI for co-evolving human and machine intelligence

The rapid evolution of artificial intelligence—particularly large language models, generative systems, and multi-agent collaboration—raises a fundamental question at the heart of science and philosophy: Will AI light up human creativity, or replace it?

Building on the foundations established in last year’s Human-Compatible AI and AI-Powered Science symposia, this symposium advances the discussion toward Well-Being AI, an approach that emphasizes the co-evolution of human and machine intelligence to maximize human potential and collective flourishing. Rather than viewing AI as isolated tools or autonomous systems, we frame AI as part of human–AI collaborative ecosystems, where creativity, knowledge, and responsibility emerge through interaction.

On the positive side, generative and reasoning systems have the potential to expand human creativity, accelerate scientific discovery, and enhance education, healthcare, and personal development. At the same time, increasing autonomy and opacity in AI systems pose serious risks, including overreliance, loss of diversity in thought, erosion of human judgment, and diminished freedom to question and choose. Trusting AI—whether imperfect or seemingly flawless—raises deep concerns when critical reflection is delegated to automated systems.

The objective of this symposium is to integrate these opportunities and risks by identifying guiding principles, evaluation methods, and design frameworks for Well-Being AI. Through interdisciplinary dialogue, we aim to clarify how AI can augment rather than replace human creativity, safeguard autonomy, and contribute to individual and social well-being. Special emphasis will be placed on synthesis and discussion sessions, including the final half day, to consolidate insights and articulate future research agendas.

### Topics

Topics include, but are not limited to: human–AI co-creation and creativity augmentation; generative and multi-agent AI; AI-powered science and discovery; interpretability, alignment, and trust; fairness, transparency, and bias mitigation; ethical, cultural, and societal implications of AI; human-centered governance and participatory design; human–AI collaborative ecosystems; metrics and evaluation for creativity and well-being; interdisciplinary approaches integrating technical, philosophical, and social perspectives.

### Format of Symposium

The symposium will be organized around invited talks, technical paper presentations, poster sessions, interactive demonstrations, and a panel discussion. Particular attention will be given to integrative and reflective sessions across the full two and a half days.

### Attendance 

The symposium is open to researchers and practitioners from artificial intelligence, computer science, human–computer interaction, ethics, psychology, neuroscience, healthcare, and related fields. No special invitation is required.

### Submission Requirements

Extended abstracts should state the intended presentation type (panel discussion, poster, or interactive demo). Accepted authors are encouraged, but not required, to present posters or interactive demonstrations in addition to oral presentations.
