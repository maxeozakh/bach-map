Monthly Researcher Note
What are we asking when we ask whether machines can be conscious?



Written by Hikari Sorensen
Recent work in LLM interpretability, particularly by Anthropic, has shown that the way LLMs organize information to model human language includes functional, i.e. causally effective, representations of emotion, and also a distinct mode/space of concept-organization that resembles the organization of (what some cognitive science theories say distinguishes) the conscious contents of human mentality. We do not yet know whether these coarse resemblances of human mental organization entail phenomenality or experience in the sense that is generally considered morally relevant. Some philosophers have an intuition that there is a special quality to biology, or to direct, continuous embeddedness in a "real" environment underlain by physics, which cannot be captured by machine simulation. But human conscious experience is itself a matter of organizing information - neither do we have access to any "real" continuous physical environment beyond our internal representation of it, and us in it, as a model that organizes our perceptions and the information made available to us by them. At CIMC we are working to understand what phenomenality is as a representational format that gives human conscious experience its particular glowing shape, distinct from any particular contents that appear within it, and whether we can interpret existing or future AI as implementing this phenomenality, whether efficiently modeling a human reality, linguistically or otherwise, possibly or necessarily converges to phenomenal representation - and if not, how this distinct representational achievement could be sparked in a machine.


Computational Consciousness in Berlin 
September 11 - Berlin
CIMC hosted Computational Consciousness in partnership with the Foresight AI Node [https://foresight.org/grants/ai-science-safety-nodes-rfp/], Zander Labs [https://www.zanderlabs.com/] and Xtropi [https://xtropi.com/?utm_source=luma]. This one-day salon brought together researchers, philosophers, builders, and funders to treat machine consciousness as an emerging domain requiring technical seriousness and ethical foresight.

Apart Research: Digital Minds Research Sprint
August 14-16 - San Francisco & Berlin
CIMC co-organized the Digital Minds Research Sprint with Apart Research, the NYU Center for Mind, Ethics & Policy, and Eleos AI Research, and hosted the San Francisco hub. Over three days, participants designed and ran experiments probing the preferences, welfare signals, introspective abilities, and identity of frontier AI models. 950 people signed up; 237 projects were submitted across six tracks.

CIMC Hackathon: Distributed Mind Sandbox Coopetition
August 1 - San Francisco
Participants built the control logic for individual organisms navigating a shared world model, balancing cooperation and competition with other participants’ organisms in real time. The challenge ran on Loom, CIMC’s distributed compute fabric, programmed as a single machine. This was Loom’s first live test.

Upcoming: CIMC Hackathon: Lattice Animals
September 25 - San Francisco
A lattice animal is a mathematician’s name for a creature made of grid cells. This month, the name becomes literal. Each participant brings a mind for a single cell, and single cells don’t count. Only animals do. To exist, an animal needs several minds to find each other, agree on a form, and hold it, with no channel but movement and no commitment but position. Hosted by Philip Rosedale and Joscha Bach.
