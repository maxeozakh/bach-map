About the Digital Minds Research Sprint
The Digital Minds Research Sprint is a global 3-day intensive program designed to explore the frontiers of artificial sentience, model preferences, AI welfare, and introspection architectures.
Throughout the weekend, participants will form teams to conduct novel research, write preliminary papers, and develop benchmarks that address how complex algorithmic systems process internal states and interact with human governance frameworks.
Research Tracks & Topics
Participants will work in teams on one of the following challenge tracks:
Track 1: Model Preferences & Trade-offs: testing the coherence and stability of model preferences, grounding choices in trade-offs (e.g. donation-equivalents), and comparing stated vs. revealed preferences.
Track 2: Distress, Flourishing & Valence Signals: mapping contexts that correlate with distress or flourishing signals in model outputs, and evaluating whether valence signals are stable across prompts and personas.
Track 3: Introspection & Self-Report Reliability: testing when models can accurately report on their internal states, comparing naive prompting against structured elicitation, and building introspection benchmarks.
Track 4: Preference Elicitation Methods: developing tools and multi-method toolkits beyond simple prompting to measure convergence across independent preference-elicitation techniques.
Track 5: The Assistant Persona & Model Identity: probing persona stability, how the assistant persona relates to the underlying base model, and whether personas mask true preferences.
Track 6: Open / Novel Considerations: an open track for participants to bring unique perspectives and propose novel research questions outside the main tracks.
CIMC researchers and engineers will actively participate in the sprint, while the broader CIMC community will provide technical mentorship on Discord to participating teams globally.
